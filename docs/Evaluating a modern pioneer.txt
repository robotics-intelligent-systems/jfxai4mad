Evaluating a modern pioneer colonization plan inspired by Joseph Smith and Brigham Young requires extracting their core organizational principles—centralized resource planning, agrarian independence, rapid community building, and distinct cultural/governance cohesion—and applying them to contemporary geopolitical, technological, and economic realities.Here is a structured evaluation of this model and the potential target regions.1. Core Model: The Smith & Young Framework in the 21st CenturyThe 19th-century pioneer migration (e.g., the State of Deseret / Great Salt Lake Basin) succeeded because of four main pillars, which require clear modern adaptations:Orderly Land & Resource Distribution:19th C.: The Plat of the City of Zion grid system; water rights managed collectively rather than speculatively.Modern Adaptation: Modular urban design, smart-grid infrastructure, communal water desalination or collection, and land trusts rather than speculative real estate.Economic Self-Reliance + Export Integration:19th C.: Local agriculture first, followed by trade along transit corridors (e.g., supply points for California gold rushers).Modern Adaptation: Agrotech (hydroponics, precision farming) for food security, combined with specialized high-value exports (e.g., specialized organic crops, carbon credits, or digital service exports).Centralized Capital & Cooperative Labor:19th C.: Perpetual Emigrating Fund, tithing system, cooperative stores (ZCMI).Modern Adaptation: Venture cooperative funding models, decentralized autonomous governance (DAOs) for asset management, and pooled capital for shared heavy infrastructure.2. Evaluation of Proposed LocationsRegion A: Ceja de Selva / Paraná Plateau (South America)Pros: High land fertility, abundant freshwater, high agricultural yield potential (soy, timber, coffee, fruit), and established inland trade corridors (Parana-Paraguay Waterway).Geopolitics & Trade: The Paraná basin sits at the heart of MERCOSUR, making it strategically placed for agricultural export to global markets (China, EU).Feasibility: High for traditional colonization. Sovereign land acquisition is straightforward via private purchase, though local environmental regulations and state taxes apply.Region B: Arequipa Valley & Peruvian High DesertPros: Highly stable climate, access to major Pacific ports (Majes-Siguas irrigation projects provide arable desert land), proximity to copper/lithium trade corridors.Geopolitics & Trade: Peru benefits from extensive Pacific Trade Agreements (CPTPP, APEC) and key infrastructure like the Port of Megapuerto de Chancay and Matarani.Feasibility: Medium-High. Agriculture requires heavy upfront irrigation/desalination infrastructure, making initial capital demands high compared to rain-fed regions.Region C: Floating Architecture (Offshore Ica, Central America, or Philippines)Pros: Complete spatial flexibility, autonomy potential, and direct access to maritime shipping lanes.Geopolitics & Trade: Seasteading in international waters or Special Economic Zones (SEZs) avoids traditional sovereign land disputes, but faces severe international maritime law challenges (UNCLOS).Feasibility: Low-Medium. High initial capital expenditure per square meter, significant exposure to extreme weather (typhoons in the Philippines, Pacific swells), and high ongoing maintenance costs. Highly dependent on import of primary materials.3.
