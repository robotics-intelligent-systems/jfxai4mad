# Extended family relationships by marriage

This note summarizes the Spanish source's examples of extended relatives connected through marriage or civil union. It is a descriptive vocabulary aid, not a legal definition or a universal genealogy standard. Meanings of terms such as *aunt-in-law*, *cousin-in-law* and *co-in-law* vary by language, family convention and jurisdiction; describe the exact relationship when precision matters.

## Relationship examples

| Relationship described | Plain-language description |
| --- | --- |
| Spouse's aunt or uncle | A sibling of one of the spouse's parents; English labels such as “aunt-in-law” and “uncle-in-law” are not used consistently. |
| Spouse's cousin | A cousin of the spouse. “Cousin-in-law” can also refer to other relationships and should be clarified. |
| Spouse's niece or nephew | A child of one of the spouse's siblings. The source calls these nieces/nephews-in-law. |
| Spouse's grandparent | A grandparent of the spouse. “Grandparent-in-law” is one possible label. |
| A sibling's spouse's relatives | The source includes relatives of the spouses of one's own siblings; the exact relation should be stated because no single English label covers every case. |

The source groups these relationships as an extended social and family network formed through a marriage or civil union, beyond the immediate in-law relationships it names. This is an informal grouping: family usage and legal recognition may differ.
