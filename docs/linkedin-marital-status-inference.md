# LinkedIn and Marital-Status Inference

LinkedIn does not provide a marital-status field that can be used to directly determine how many users are single, married or in another relationship status.

## 1. Method used in the original document

The plaintext file combined:

- estimates of LinkedIn profiles by region;
- an estimated share of women on the platform;
- general unmarried-population rates;
- aggregate international-tourism figures.

Those variables were then used to estimate the number of single/unmarried women present on LinkedIn.

## 2. Methodological correction

That calculation **must not be interpreted as an observed LinkedIn attribute**. Applying a general demographic rate to a platform population introduces major biases and cannot determine the marital status of a specific person.

### Do not use this inference for

- individual segmentation;
- candidate selection;
- hiring decisions;
- prospecting for personal relationships;
- inferring romantic availability.

## 3. Separation of metrics

Tourism metrics included in the original document should remain separate from professional-platform data. A regional correlation among tourism, LinkedIn use and marital status does not establish causation and cannot support individual behavioral inferences.

## 4. Recommendation

For professional LinkedIn analysis, use only legitimate, relevant and transparently available professional attributes, respecting platform rules and applicable privacy law.
