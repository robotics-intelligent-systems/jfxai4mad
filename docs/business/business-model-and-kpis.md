# Business Model and Performance Indicators

## Commercial model

The project proposes monetization of delivered services rather than intimate data or vulnerability.

| Revenue stream | Proposed offering | Validation required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LegalTech coordination | Disclosed fees for case preparation and administrative support | Permitted scope, provider responsibilities and refund terms |
| Destination tourism | Disclosed commissions or revenue sharing | Signed supplier agreements, cancellation conditions and unit economics |
| B2B subscriptions | Operator tools for availability, events and workflow coordination | Willingness to pay and licensing model |
| Premium concierge | Travel assistance, translation and document-service coordination | Supplier qualifications, service limits and delivery times |

## KPI framework

| KPI | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Completed-service conversion | Completed agreed service packages / accepted proposals, with cancellations reported separately |
| Legal-processing time | Elapsed time by case stage, separating platform handling from authority processing |
| Reciprocal opt-in | Mutually approved interactions / eligible introduction requests |
| User and provider satisfaction | Post-service feedback with response rate, sample size and incident outcomes |
| Contribution margin | Revenue minus direct supplier, support, payment and refund costs |

These are proposed measurements, not published performance results.

## Evaluation rules

- Report denominators and sample sizes.
- Separate operational completion from user satisfaction.
- Do not treat conversion rate as a proxy for user welfare or consent quality.
- Keep personal discovery metrics separate from professional collaboration metrics.
- Recalculate supplier economics before entering a new jurisdiction.
- Label historical cost and conversion estimates as hypotheses unless backed by current data.
