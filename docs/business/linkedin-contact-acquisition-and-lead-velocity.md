# LinkedIn Contact Acquisition and Lead Velocity

English consolidation of the supplemental Spanish source. This is a capacity scenario, not an observed acquisition forecast. Read it alongside [ten-job payroll and B2B conversion](ten-skilled-jobs-payroll-and-b2b-conversion.md).

## Time to reach 30,000 contacts

The source states that adding 100 potential customers per week takes exactly 300 weeks, approximately 5.77 years or five years and nine months.

For a starting inventory of zero, no duplicates or losses, and a constant **net addition** rate:

`weeks = ceiling((30,000 - existing unique contacts) / net new unique contacts per week)`

Using 52 weeks per year gives 300 / 52 = 5.7692 years. Calendar durations are approximate; this calculation does not prove that 100 invitations generate 100 accepted connections or qualified prospects.

## Source acceleration scenarios

| Weekly capacity | Source mechanism | Weeks | Approximate equivalent |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 100 | One individual account; described as a native limit | 300 | 5.8 years |
| 250 | One account plus automation/InMail | 120 | 2.3 years |
| 500 | Team of five people/accounts | 60 | 1.15 years; about 14 months |
| 1,000 | Team of ten people or B2B campaigns | 30 | About seven months |

All four divisions are correct under the stated net-addition assumption. The mechanisms are translated source proposals, not verified throughput. InMail messages, invitations, accepted connections, identified prospects and qualified leads must be tracked separately. Team totals require deduplication across accounts and companies.

## LinkedIn limits and channel distinctions

The source asserts an average weekly allowance of 100–200 connection requests per personal profile. It proposes multiple SDR/sales profiles, Sales Navigator InMail and organic content/GitHub/PLG to shorten the timeline without risking accounts.

**Selected official checks, 2026-10-12:** LinkedIn's [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/invitation-restrictions) confirm invitation limits and a maximum of 30,000 first-degree connections per member. The checked article does not publish a universal 100–200 weekly allowance. That source range and the table's “native limit” remain unverified.

LinkedIn's [software policy](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) prohibits third-party software that automates website activity, fake accounts and bypassing use limits. The automation row is retained for source fidelity, not recommended as an authorized growth mechanism. Team members' legitimate individual networks do not establish a pooled account allowance or exemption from restrictions. InMail is a separate communication channel, not an accepted connection.

The contact inventory in this business scenario is distinct from LinkedIn's individual network ceiling. No account, message or invitation was created.

## Consolidation with the payroll scenario

The earlier note assumes a 30,000-contact starting universe. This supplement adds the time needed to build that universe from zero; acquisition and subsequent sales-cycle delays must be included in the funding runway. If contacts already exist, use the remaining gap rather than rebuilding the full inventory.

At 100 invitations/week and a hypothetical 15% acceptance rate, accepted connections grow by 15/week, implying 2,000 weeks from zero, before duplicates or losses. This is an editorial illustration, not a forecast: the earlier 15% stage combines acceptance and initial response, so it is not a measured acceptance rate.

Report gross channel activity, unique net contacts, qualification, paid conversion and cash receipts separately. The [business KPI framework](business-model-and-kpis.md) and [investment roadmap](../roadmap/implementation-and-investment.md) provide the related measurement and funding context.
