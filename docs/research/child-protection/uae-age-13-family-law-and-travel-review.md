# UAE age-13 source review: family law and child travel

This note processes an additional Spanish draft about a girl aged thirteen or
younger. The draft inconsistently alternates between “under thirteen” and “aged
thirteen”; those are different age bands. This is child-protection and legal
research, not an authorisation, a marriage service or an itinerary.

Selected primary-source checks: 11 October 2026. The expression “minimum age of
authorisation” has no single meaning: marriage capacity, custody consent, border
documents and carrier acceptance must be evaluated separately.

## 1. Marriage claims and legal scope

| Source assertion | Review outcome |
| --- | --- |
| General marriage threshold of 18 Gregorian years under Law 41/2024 | Official material supports the ordinary threshold and a court-permission exception; call the instrument Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2024. |
| No registrar or court processes any under-18 contract | Too absolute: the source itself mentions court exceptions. Ordinary registration and judicial exceptions are distinct. |
| Guardian signature alone establishes permission | Not supported; the reviewed official material requires court involvement for the exception. |
| Courts never approve cases at thirteen or below | Not established by the evidence retrieved. Do not present an assumed court practice as an absolute statutory minimum or promise approval. |
| Every unofficial attempt automatically incurs imprisonment/fines for facilitators and guardians | No specific offence, elements or sentencing provision was verified for this blanket claim. Retain as unverified, not an operational rule. |
| Civil marriage has one nationwide “18 or 21” threshold, without exceptions | Jurisdiction and legal regime must be identified; the alternatives cannot be used interchangeably. |

The [official government summary of Sharia marriage](https://u.ae/information-and-services/social-affairs/marriages/marriage-as-per-the-sharia-law)
and indexed [Decree-Law 41/2024](https://www.uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/2770/download)
support eighteen Gregorian years as the ordinary threshold and refer to court
permission and applicable controls. Indexed [Cabinet Resolution 212/2025](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/4042/download)
confirms a further regulatory instrument on under-eighteen marriage.
Its complete text was not retrieved in this review; no thirteen-specific minimum
or judicial outcome was verified.

The [Abu Dhabi Civil Family Court](https://www.adjd.gov.ae/EN/Pages/CivilFamilyCourt.aspx)
lists eighteen as its service minimum. This is a jurisdiction-specific reference;
the federal civil regime under Decree-Law 41/2022 requires separate checking.
The draft's twenty-one alternative remains unresolved here. None of the checked
ordinary civil-service conditions establishes eligibility for a thirteen-year-old.

## 2. Carrier acceptance is not legal travel permission

The draft claims that Emirates, Etihad and flydubai universally require an
Unaccompanied Minor (UM) service at thirteen. Their published policies contradict
that generalisation.

| Carrier | Younger passengers | At thirteen | Primary reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emirates | UM service for solo children aged 5–11 | Can travel without UM; service can be requested for 12–15 | [Emirates UM policy](https://www.emirates.com/english/family/planning-your-family-trip/unaccompanied-minors/) |
| Etihad | Under 5 must be accompanied; solo 5–11 require the arranged UM service | Solo travel on adult ticket permitted for 12–17; UM optional | [Etihad UM policy](https://www.etihad.com/en-us/manage/special-assistance/unaccompanied-minors) |
| flydubai | Under 12 must have an accompanying eligible passenger; no UM service offered | Age 12+ may travel alone with required route documents | [flydubai FAQ](https://www.flydubai.com/en/help/faq?fc=Travelling+with+children&fn=flydubai) and [conditions §7.7](https://www.flydubai.com/en/information/policies/conditions-of-carriage) |

Emirates uses “over 12” in some prose while explicitly describing optional service
for 12–15; the table's age-thirteen conclusion is unambiguous. Flydubai's
conditions also contain slightly different boundary wording; recheck the precise
age-twelve case rather than generalising this age-thirteen comparison.

“Under thirteen” includes infants, under-five children, 5–11 and twelve-year-olds.
A single UM rule cannot represent that population. Operating carrier, connections
and route documents can impose additional requirements.

UM airport assistance and handover do not demonstrate a transfer of **complete
legal custody**, as claimed by the draft. The reviewed carrier material describes
assistance and supervision, not replacement of parental or court authority.

## 3. Custody consent, notarisation and travel restrictions

The draft claims every child under thirteen needs a notarised father's NOC to
leave the UAE; that mothers must always carry this document plus a birth
certificate; that divorced mothers need paternal or judicial permission; and
that either parent can directly register a border-system travel ban.

These are retained as claims requiring legal-regime and procedural verification:

- Indexed Decree-Law 41/2024 includes custody-travel and passport provisions
  (Articles 116–117). Its retrieved excerpt supports written consent requirements
  for non-parent custodians and court involvement in specified circumstances.
  The complete parental-custody travel provision was not retrieved here.
- [Civil regulations, Resolution 122/2023](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/2301/download)
  distinguish joint-custody travel disputes and applications to the competent
  court for temporary prevention of travel (Articles 21–22, indexed excerpt).
  A parent's ability to **apply** is not an automatic border ban.
- No current GDRFA rule establishing the draft's universal notarised paternal
  NOC and birth-certificate checklist was verified. Do not infer it from a
  family-law consent provision or an airline form.
- Notarisation, applicable guardian, court orders, identity documents and
  destination/transit requirements must be checked for the actual situation.
  Carrier acceptance alone does not establish parental consent or lawful exit.

The legal references above were reviewed through official search excerpts;
direct portal/PDF retrieval failed (403/internal errors). They establish limited
research findings, not a complete current-law determination.
