# Lima–Rio de Janeiro bus travel: route and fare research

Source: `docs/El viaje en autobús de.txt`, migration entry 61. Reviewed on 10 October 2026.

This English note preserves the Spanish travel draft and separates its estimates from selected primary-source checks. For destination event dates, see the [Rio Carnival calendar](rio-carnival-calendar.md).

## Translated route summary

The draft calls Lima–Rio the world's longest continuous bus journey, linking South America's Pacific and Atlantic coasts. Trans Acreana also advertises a world-record route; no independent Guinness record was checked for this note.

## Translated itinerary and geographic qualification

1. **Peruvian coast:** Lima southward through Ica and Nazca.
2. **Andes and Cusco:** Abancay and Cusco, then Puerto Maldonado. Puerto Maldonado is in the Amazon lowlands, not the Andean highlands.
3. **Peru–Brazil border:** Iñapari to Assis Brasil in Acre.
4. **Amazon and Brazilian interior:** Rio Branco and Porto Velho, continuing through Mato Grosso and Goiás according to the draft.
5. **Southeastern Brazil:** São Paulo, followed by Rio de Janeiro.

This is a translated route hypothesis, not a confirmed stop-by-stop timetable. The operator's Rio-to-Peru page lists Rio, São Paulo, Campo Grande (Mato Grosso do Sul), Cuiabá (Mato Grosso), Porto Velho and Rio Branco as boarding/alighting cities. It does not list Goiás or the Peruvian stops above. Do not silently treat the two itineraries as equivalent; request the actual direction-specific route.

## Translated onboard services

The source describes *Leito* or *Semi-Leito* reclining seats, USB ports/power outlets, Wi-Fi subject to coverage, air conditioning and an onboard toilet. It says roadside restaurants provide two or three daily stops for meals and personal hygiene.

The operator page mentions filtered water, Wi-Fi and three daily hygiene/meal stops. Seat class, sockets, air conditioning, toilet, food inclusion and continuous connectivity were not confirmed for a selected departure. Verify the assigned vehicle and ticket conditions.

## Translated practical advice and documentation scope

- **Documents:** the draft recommends a valid passport or national ID for MERCOSUR/Andean Community nationals. That blanket regional statement is too broad. The published Brazil–Peru agreement, promulgated by Decree 5,537/2005, provides for the listed national identity documents for citizens of those two countries travelling for tourism or business. Entry, exit and other immigration requirements still apply; it is not a rule for every Andean nationality, residence or travel purpose.
- **Meals and incidental costs:** the source recommends carrying soles and Brazilian reais for roadside spending. Retain this as planning advice; meal inclusion and payment acceptance need confirmation.
- **Reservation:** the source recommends booking several weeks ahead for Carnival or year-end travel through the operator's official website or agency. The claimed high demand is not quantified; availability and fares require a dated quotation.
