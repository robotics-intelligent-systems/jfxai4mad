# Women in the STEM Education-to-Career Pipeline

English translation and evidence review of `El análisis de mujeres.txt`. **Status:** background note; global indicators below are source-dated, while several inherited claims lack a traceable source or a defined population.

## Global participation indicators

UNESCO reports that women represent about **35% of tertiary STEM graduates globally**. This is a broad field aggregate: it should not be read as the share in every STEM discipline, country or graduating cohort. UNESCO also reports that women are **26% of employees in data and artificial intelligence** in the cited global summary. The education and employment measures have different populations and should not be treated as a single transition rate. [UNESCO: Closing the digital divide for women and girls through education](https://www.unesco.org/en/gender-equality/education/digital-divide)

The source's claims that approximately 79% of secondary-school girls are enthusiastic about technology/science, only 16% ultimately select a STEM degree, STEM explains around 30% of future income and growth gaps in emerging economies, and women hold 12% of global STEM management posts versus 27.5% in non-STEM sectors are **not substantiated in the supplied note**. No study, date, country set, question wording, denominator, or definition of “STEM leadership” is supplied. Do not use these as project baselines or compare them directly.

## Retention and career progression

The source describes a “leaky pipeline,” reduced confidence, gender stereotypes, care responsibilities, pay gaps and a transition problem around ages 25–35. These are plausible topics for investigation, but the cited age band and claimed abrupt exit are not established by the source. “Leaving STEM,” changing occupation, moving into management, taking a career break, and leaving paid employment are distinct outcomes and need separate measurement.

UNESCO's global review identifies gender stereotypes and unequal conditions as barriers and calls for fair working conditions for women graduates. UNESCO's latest research-indicator summary also shows that women's representation differs across education, research sectors and senior scientific leadership. These sources support investigating progression barriers, not a single universal age threshold or causal explanation. [UNESCO: G20 call for greater gender equality in science](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/g20-unesco-calls-greater-gender-equality-science) · [UNESCO UIS: Measuring gender equality in science](https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/news/measuring-gender-equality-science-why-better-data-matter)

## Intervention options retained from the source

- **Mentorship and role models:** test voluntary mentoring that connects early-career participants with experienced practitioners. Define a useful outcome (for example, skills confidence, access to professional networks, or retention) and evaluate it rather than assuming effect.
- **Funding:** consider scholarships and research/development grants through transparent, published eligibility and selection criteria. Verify applicable funding rules and assess access barriers.
- **Emerging skills:** offer short courses or certificates in AI, software, data analysis and renewable energy only after demand, quality, accessibility and placement outcomes are assessed.

These are proposed interventions, not services already offered or proven results. Participation should be voluntary; selection should use documented, role-relevant criteria and protect personal data.

## Proposed evidence task: MAD-STEM-001

Build a dated evidence matrix for named countries and cohorts. Record source, year, population, numerator/denominator, field classification, education level, occupation, employment status, seniority, pay measure, and uncertainty. Use UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS) indicators for sex-disaggregated tertiary graduates by field and R&D personnel, and harmonized labor data where available. Do not compare “graduates,” “workers,” “researchers,” and “leaders” as if they were interchangeable. UIS warns that field definitions and coverage can limit cross-country comparability. [UIS Data Browser](https://databrowser.uis.unesco.org/) · [UIS: graduates by field of education](https://uis.unesco.org/node/3079560)

No personal records, recruitment, scholarship allocation, or intervention evaluation were performed in this note.
