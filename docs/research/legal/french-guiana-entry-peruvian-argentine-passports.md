# French Guiana Entry: Peruvian and Argentine Passports

**Editorial review:** 9 October 2026 (Lima).  
**Status:** translated research note with selected official-source checks; not an individual admission decision.

This note translates the four sections of plaintext source entry 57 and consolidates the repeated document and health questions from entry 56. The [access note](../tourism/french-guiana-air-and-overland-access.md) retains the air and river/bridge alternatives. Both originals remain in Git history and are mapped in the [migration register](../../ANNEX-DOCS-CONSOLIDATED.md#10-complete-migration-and-translation-register).

“Compatibility” in the source means travel-document eligibility for a person with Peruvian and Argentine nationality. It has no connection to the project's social-matching compatibility algorithms.

## 1. Territorial framework

The source describes French Guiana as a French overseas department/region with a distinct border regime outside Schengen. France-Visas confirms that non-European French territories have territorial visa rules; a Schengen visa alone is not general authorization for an overseas territory. French Guiana is an EU outermost region; being outside Schengen should not be rewritten as being outside the EU. [FG-02, FG-01](#shared-reference-register)

## 2. Nationality and short-stay exemption

| Passport in the source | Source assertion | Selected legal check |
|---|---|---|
| Argentine | Visa-exempt tourism for up to 90 days; valid passport, onward ticket and funds | The consolidated overseas decree's Annex II lists Argentina with an exemption extending to all passport types [FG-01](#shared-reference-register) |
| Peruvian | The same visa-exempt tourism assertion | The consolidated Annex II also lists Peru with an exemption extending to all passport types [FG-01](#shared-reference-register) |

Use the **consolidated text**, not only the original July 2011 publication: the original had narrower Peruvian exemptions. The version surfaced during review reports a last update of 19 June 2025. Check the applicable version, purpose and personal circumstances before relying on it. The source's “90 days in 180” statement is retained as its short-stay claim; the selected nationality-table check does not independently determine every stay-counting or transit condition.

The draft attributes the exemption to two agreements and calls dual nationality “fully compatible.” The selected references establish the territorial exemption entries; they do not establish that bilateral-agreement explanation or a blanket no-conflict rule for every journey. Exemption from a short-stay visa does not decide admission, work or residence eligibility. Dual nationality should not be used to infer an additional allowance of days.

## 3. Entry documents and health

### Translation of the five document groups

| Accommodation | Confirmed hotel booking or formal attestation d'accueil | Retain alternatives from the draft; determine the applicable document for the actual lodging arrangement |

### Yellow fever: evidence and qualifications

Institut Pasteur states that travelers aged **one year and over** need a yellow-fever certificate on entry to French Guiana regardless of origin, and gives a minimum **ten-day** lead time for a first vaccination; it describes administrative certificate validity as lifelong. The source's ten-day claim is therefore qualified by age and first-vaccination context. [FG-03](#shared-reference-register)

The prefecture's health page also recognizes a **medical-contraindication certificate** pathway. Consequently, “no yellow card always means denied boarding” is not carried forward as a universal rule. Age, contraindication documentation and carrier/authority acceptance require individual confirmation; this note neither recommends vaccination for a particular person nor adjudicates an exemption. [FG-04](#shared-reference-register)

The source's claim that Brazil and Suriname necessarily demand a physical certificate is not verified by these French Guiana references. Each transit country's rule and acceptable document format must be checked independently.

## 4. Dual-nationality document handling

The source's “golden rule” says to use the same passport for airline check-in and entry/exit and warns of discrepancies when switching mid-route. Keep it as an identity-consistency concern rather than a universal legal command. A carrier booking, a country's entry record and obligations toward a country of nationality can have different document rules; establish a plan with each relevant authority/carrier and retain consistent identity information.

The draft states there is no need for a prior consular visa using either passport. The nationality check supports the listed short-stay exemptions, not every activity or transit. An itinerary via mainland France, Brazil or Suriname needs its own checks; a French Guiana exemption does not settle those legs. [FG-01, FG-02](#shared-reference-register)
