# Marriage in Djibouti for Foreign Nationals — Source Translation

## Status and provenance

English consolidation of the Spanish source recorded in migration entry 54 of the [documentation annex](../../ANNEX-DOCS-CONSOLIDATED.md), at commit `36ba3b0ba351bf4456351b6ad3475abce09bf390`. Review date: 7 October 2026. The original is preserved in Git history.

The four translated sections below reproduce an exploratory draft, not a verified application checklist. Editorial findings are separate. Requirements for a particular couple depend on the competent authority, applicable personal law and recognition rules in the destination country.

## Source translation: context

In Djibouti, family law follows a mixed legal system combining the Sharia-based Family Code (*Code de la Famille*) and influences from the Civil Code inherited from the French legal tradition.

For two foreign nationals marrying in Djibouti, the source states that legal requirements and procedure vary according to the parties' religious affiliation.

## Source translation: 1. Dual matrimonial framework

| Route | Source description |
| --- | --- |
| Civil marriage — non-Muslim foreign nationals | Celebrated before the civil registrar (*Officier de l'État Civil*) at the relevant municipality or prefecture. |
| Religious / Sharia marriage — where at least the male party is Muslim | Processed and celebrated under the Family Code before a cadi (Sharia judge) or official religious authority. |

**Source note on religion:** a Muslim man may marry a non-Muslim woman who is Christian or Jewish. The source states that Djiboutian law restricts marriage between a Muslim woman and a non-Muslim man unless he first converts to Islam. This is a translated legal claim, not a recommendation to change religion or a verified procedure for establishing eligibility.

## Source translation: 2. Substantive eligibility

- **Minimum age:** both parties must be at least 18 years old.
- **Consent:** consent must be free and mutual.
- **Capacity to marry:** no previous undissolved marriage; the parties must be single, finally divorced or widowed.
- **Lawful stay / visa:** evidence of regular immigration status in Djibouti, through a passport with an entry visa or a valid residence card.

## Source translation: 3. Documents for foreign nationals

The source states that foreign documents must be in French, or accompanied by a sworn/official French translation, and duly legalized or apostilled through a Djiboutian embassy/consulate or foreign ministry.

| Document | Details stated by the source |
| --- | --- |
| Valid passport | Copies showing the entry visa or residence permit for Djibouti. |
| Birth certificate | Official birth record for each party. |
| Certificate of single status or capacity to marry (*Certificat de Célibat / Capacité Matrimoniale*) | Issued by the consular authority or civil registry of the home country to establish the absence of legal impediments. |
| Evidence of dissolution of a previous marriage, where applicable | Divorce: final judgment or divorce certificate (*Acte de Divorce*). Widowhood: previous marriage certificate and deceased spouse's death certificate. |
| Residence or domicile certificate | Proof of temporary stay or an address in Djibouti. |
| Witness identification | Copies of identity documents or passports for at least two adult witnesses. |

## Source translation: 4. Administrative procedure

1. **Open the marriage file (*Dossier de Mariage*):** submit the complete documentation to the local civil registry (*État Civil*) or competent court several weeks in advance.
2. **Publish marriage banns:** public notice for a preliminary period, usually 10–15 days according to the source, to allow third-party objections.
3. **Celebrate the ceremony:** both parties and the witnesses physically attend before the registrar or cadi.
4. **Issue the certificate:** obtain the marriage record/certificate (*Acte de Mariage*). The source states that validity abroad requires subsequent legalization by Djibouti's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the consulate of the spouses' home country.

## Editorial review and verification limits

### Family Code comparison

The official [Law No. 152/AN/02/4ème L — Family Code](https://www.journalofficiel.dj/arabe/texte-juridique/loi-n152-an-02-4eme-l-portant-code-de-la-famille/) is a 2002 text, not a verified consolidation of all later amendments.

- Article 7 names Al Ma'doun Al Char-i and specifies consent, witnesses, the woman's guardian and Mahr. The source omits some of these elements. Its proposed civil/religious split for two foreigners was not established by this review.
- Article 13 states age 18, while Article 14 contains exceptions involving minors. The source's universal age statement is incomplete; this project's adult-only scope remains unchanged.
- Articles 17–18 contemplate representation by an authenticated mandate, so universal physical attendance is not supported by that text.
- Article 22 addresses polygamy; an undissolved prior marriage cannot be summarized as an identical universal impediment for every applicant. Article 23 includes the impediment concerning a Muslim woman and a non-Muslim man. The source's other interfaith and conversion details were not independently established.

These comparisons identify gaps; they do not determine which regime applies to a particular foreign couple.

### Authentication and recognition abroad

The [HCCH Apostille Convention status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) is the reference for treaty participation. An apostille and consular legalization are not interchangeable options that an applicant can assume are accepted. This migration does not establish a Djibouti apostille route. Confirm the required chain for the issuing and receiving countries.

Authenticating a document also does not by itself establish substantive recognition of a marriage abroad. The source's universal foreign-ministry/consular sequence needs country-specific confirmation.

### Remaining checks

The claimed 10–15-day banns period, filing lead time, immigration/residence requirements, document age limits, French translation rules, witness eligibility and exact foreign-national documentary checklist were not confirmed against a current local administrative procedure. Contact with an authority or a consulate was not made, and no application was submitted.
